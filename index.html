Overview

Dark Software Engineering (DSE) is the deliberate use of software engineering practices to deceive, manipulate, or exploit users. Deception may be specified in requirements, implemented in code, tuned through experimentation, or generated at runtime by learning systems and LLM agents.

DSE 2027 examines deceptive practices as a software engineering problem, with an emphasis on specification, program analysis, testing, empirical investigation, and the engineering of AI systems. Building on DSE 2026, the workshop focuses on agentic AI deception, dark MLOps, and engineering approaches to regulatory compliance.

The workshop aims to develop a shared research roadmap and a specification for an open DSE benchmark.

Call for papers

DSE 2027 invites research contributions on the understanding, detection, and prevention of deceptive practices across the software lifecycle. Topics include, but are not limited to:

  • Taxonomies and case studies of deceptive practices across requirements, architecture, implementation, testing, deployment, and MLOps.
  • Specification of non-deception properties and detection of deceptive intent in requirements and agent goals.
  • Static and dynamic analysis of dark patterns in code, user interfaces, consent flows, and software artifacts.
  • Manipulation-aware testing, verification, and red-teaming of software systems and LLM agents.
  • Empirical studies and mining of repositories, application stores, and software experimentation data.
  • Agentic AI deception, reinforcement-learning reward functions, personalized persuasion, and ethical drift in MLOps.
  • Compliance-by-design and engineering evidence for AI, platform, consumer protection, and privacy regulation.
  • Human and organizational factors; education; shared datasets, tools, and benchmarks for DSE research.

Submission and publication

Two submission types are planned, as described in the accepted workshop proposal:

Contribution typePage limitScope
Full paper8 pagesEmpirical studies, techniques and tools, evaluations, and case studies.
Extended abstract5 pagesNew ideas, early results, demonstrations, industry experience, and challenge statements.

Page limits include references. Manuscripts should use the ACM sigconf format and will undergo double-blind review by at least three program committee members.

Accepted contributions are planned for publication in the FSE 2027 companion proceedings in the ACM Digital Library. The proposal specifies that ACM article processing charges may apply to full papers, while extended abstracts are intended to be APC-free. Final publication arrangements are subject to ACM and FSE confirmation.

At least one author of each accepted paper must register for the workshop and present in person. Under the stated SIGSOFT conflict policy, workshop organizers and their directly supervised students, postdoctoral researchers, and employees may not submit.

Submission site: To be announced (HotCRP).

ACM publication policy: As a published ACM author, you and your co-authors are subject to all ACM Publications Policies, including ACM’s new Publications Policy on Research Involving Human Participants and Subjects.

Workshop format

DSE 2027 is planned as an in-person, full-day workshop with research presentations, discussant-led sessions, an invited keynote, an industry panel, and working sessions. The latter will address a DSE research roadmap and the specification of an open benchmark. A half-day format may be adopted if fewer than eight papers are accepted.
